The story

From emergency medicine to a ministry of support.

For fifteen years, Dr. Richard Niemeyer and his wife Jean travelled to mission fields all over the world, wherever there was an emergency and a need for a doctor. At home in Pennsylvania he ran his own medical practice in Leola.

In 1985 he founded Youth Outreach International as a 501(c)(3) charity, and it let them reach far more people. Through it they provided medical help and medicines, clinics, AIDS test kits, food and supplies, along with education in preventing disease. The purpose was always to support the work of others who were spreading the gospel of Jesus Christ.

No need was too challenging, whether it was clean water, food, solar power or something else. YOI sent many 40-foot shipping containers filled with all kinds of critically needed supplies, as well as millions of nutritious rice packets. It also helped produce and distribute Project Peanut Butter, a lifesaving food that has saved the lives of countless malnourished children under two years old.

Dr. Niemeyer’s master’s degree in public health gave him access to government officials and health departments in a number of countries, where he helped plan programs to prevent disease.

Over the years YOI has served many people and organizations around the world, always helping others who share its mission and are dedicated to evangelism and discipleship. Again and again, what impressed the Niemeyers most was the capability and passion of national Christians. They already know the culture, the language and the needs of their own people, and can work on a much lower budget than outsiders. They still greatly appreciate, and need, outside help to expand their outreaches.

The Niemeyers no longer travel, but that conviction is how YOI works today, through three ministries they have known for years. Meet the ministries →
