History

Youth Outreach International (YOI) has been a registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization since 1985. It was founded by Dr. Richard Niemeyer, who, together with his wife Jean, had already spent 15 years serving in emergency situations on mission fields around the world. While maintaining his medical practice in Leola, Pennsylvania, Dr. Niemeyer responded wherever there was a need for medical care, bringing both physical assistance and Christian compassion to those facing crisis.

The establishment of YOI greatly expanded their ability to serve others. Through the generosity of donors and partners, YOI provided medical assistance, medicines, clinics, AIDS test kits, food, emergency supplies, clean water projects, solar solutions, and preventive health education. Their goal was to support and strengthen the work of organizations and individuals who were faithfully sharing the Gospel of Jesus Christ. No challenge was considered too great if it could help meet critical human needs and open doors for ministry.

Over the years, YOI shipped numerous 40-foot containers filled with essential supplies to communities around the world and distributed millions of nutritious rice packets to help combat hunger. The organization also played an important role in the production and distribution of The Peanut Butter Project, a highly effective nutritional supplement that helped save the lives of countless severely malnourished children under the age of two. In addition, Dr. Niemeyer’s Master of Public Health degree enabled him to work alongside government officials and health departments in various countries to develop preventive health programs and improve community health outcomes.

Throughout its history, YOI has partnered with many ministries and organizations that share its commitment to evangelism, discipleship, and compassionate outreach. The organization has been especially encouraged by the dedication and effectiveness of national Christian leaders who serve within their own cultures and communities. Because they understand the language, customs, and needs of their people, they are uniquely positioned to carry out ministry efficiently and effectively. While these local ministries often operate on modest budgets, they continue to benefit greatly from partnerships that help expand their reach and impact.
