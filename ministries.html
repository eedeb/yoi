Webuye, western Kenya  ·  Pastor Elyves Situma

Grace Life Bible College

    No photographs here yet.

    Grace Life Bible College is in Webuye, western Kenya. The founder and president is Pastor Elyves Situma. A quote from their website, www.glbc.co.ke, says our mission is “equipping students with biblical knowledge, spiritual growth, and practical skills for ministry. We offer Theology, Church Music and Computer courses to empower you for God’s purpose.” “Our curriculum is grounded upon the authority of the inerrant Word of God.”

    Youth Outreach has worked with this passionate, creative man of prayer, Pastor Situma, for many years, entertaining him and his wife in our home and helping him when he is in the States to gather shipments of supplies to send back to Kenya. Twice now he has filled a container with a total of over 26,000 Christian books for his library, which has enabled the college to get certified as a college in Kenya. In addition, our donors helped him get a supply of tools to enable students and graduates to have side jobs. He also was able to get technical equipment and music equipment. YOI has supported his ministry with finances, prayer, and frequent texts, phone calls and photos. The very best way to understand this college is to refer to their website. The drop-downs in the website show a lot of information about its doctrinal statement, history, programs, etc. One of the degrees is a seminary degree and graduates about 20 pastors a year.

    God planted into Pastor Situma’s heart the idea of a Biblical training college from statistics presented at an African Evangelical Association (AEA) conference that gave a conservative estimate that 85–90% of pastors and church leaders in Africa have little to no biblical and theological training required to effectively disciple their congregations.

    During Covid, Pastor Situma was not idle. He had up to 18 extra children in his home whose parents brought them to him because they could not feed them. When businesses were allowed to start back up, he shared his resources with people to replenish their shops and get back on their feet. But more than that, God gave him a vision to take sound doctrinal teaching to the remote villages. He wrote the curriculum, recruited several of his seminary graduates, and Youth Outreach donors bought 2 motorcycles. Enthusiasm in the villages was overwhelming, and the demand for classes grew quickly to 14 but had to be cut back to 8 due to the lack of resources. The class size is limited to a pastor and 40 of their congregants, including those who help with church leadership. The teachers stay for 2 days in one village, teaching classes and holding meetings individually with the leadership. They also check the homework. If the student attends regularly and does all the homework, he or she receives a certificate. This course has created so much interest in the college that the college has had to add dorm and classroom space to accommodate the influx. The teachers eat and sleep in the village and then move on to their second village of the week. They receive about $6.00 a day if funds are available. After 4 days they return to the college to study and prepare lessons, copy notebooks and handouts, and service the motorbikes and other necessary preparations. They may also preach in a church on Sunday and work a side job to supplement the income. They often have families to support.

    The needs here are many.

    The college needs to grow in all ways, including more teachers and staff. (They welcome experienced pastors to come teach for several weeks at a time.) They are renewing their accreditation, which is an expensive process. YOI donors have helped send 2 40-foot containers with over 26,000 books, along with shelves, tools for side jobs for some of the students, musical instruments, medical and technical equipment, and technical supplies. Also the village ministry could easily expand with another motorcycle ($2500) and more for wages for more teachers and more supplies for the classes.

    Montrouis, Haiti  ·  Pastor Pierre Gesner Fedelus

    Institution Puits Jacob/Pistos

      No photographs here yet.

      One of the national pastors YOI supports is in Haiti and runs a school for about 600 children in Montrouis, which is between St. Mark and Port-au-Prince, and was founded in 2015. Rich and Jean have known Pastor Pierre Gesner Fedelus (called Pastor Gesner) for years from their work in Haiti and the pastor’s visits to the States, where he stayed with them several times in their home.

      Pastor Gesner is totally devoted to a solid Christian education – and years ago started the school for students through grade 12 who come in daily from the mountains and towns. Pastor Gesner is a hard-working man who is dedicated to his students, staff, congregation and to his family. He has a very strong belief in prayer and faith in God to meet their needs. Occasionally, Pastor Gesner comes to the States to preach to raise support and recently linked up with a missionary aviation group who will fly in some supplies.

      The school is fully accredited and is thought to be one of the best in the area. Recently 65% of the graduates passed the government achievement exams, and 100% of the 9th graders passed theirs! Pastor Gesner also recently started a computer class with computers donated from the States.

      Life in Haiti has always been hard, but the current situation of gangs overtaking cities by looting and with deadly violence has caused unbelievable hardship. Recently the gangs came onto the school property and stole the only car. Pastor Gesner moved his family further north, but he and the staff continue to teach. He goes back to his family by bus, which is unsafe, only one day a week. Some students are afraid to even come to the school, so a few brave teachers have gone out to the students’ homes in the mountains to teach, realizing the importance of education. Because of this, in 2026, Pastor Gesner opened a second school in this outlying location.

      The school charges tuition, but the difficulty is that many parents cannot find work, so they have no way to feed their children or pay tuition. But they are allowed to come anyway. Students often come to school so weak that they cannot sit up or study. YOI donors help the school provide one plain rice meal 3 times a week without anything added, like vegetables, protein or vitamins. Statistics claim that 5.7 million Haitians suffer severe food insecurity, which includes 1.2 million children under 5.

      In order to help these families become self-sustaining, YOI has helped create some solutions. There has already been a microfinance program for a number of years. Pastor Gesner and his staff proposed a goat program. He has had a few years of experience raising goats plus he has some law experience so he can make legal contracts with each recipient, and he has trained with Hope International on how to run the microfinance program.

      This fall, because of a special grant we received, 20 families have each been given one pregnant goat. The grant also supplies 2 paid supervisors to run the program, 2 male goats, veterinary services, and other supplies. The goats will be kept at the recipient’s home. Any milk produced can be used by the family. According to the contracts, the new-born “kids” will be moved along to new participants, and if twins are born, the original participant can keep one to increase its own flock. This will not produce enough income to pay a lot of tuition, but it should help buy some food and provide a little extra income to start up other businesses. The families also plan to collect the animal waste to help fertilize the poor soil to try to grow some food around their homes.

      The needs here are many.

      They have a grant to fully fund the budget of $4,000 for this first goat project. If it is successful, more Haitians will want to duplicate the program for which they will need more start up funds.

      Food is a huge need for 600 students and 40 staff. Right now they try to supply plain rice with no protein or vegetables or oil three times a week. For about $2,500 a month, which is about $3.90 per person per month they could do better. YOI would like to increase their monthly donation in order to supply more meals. In the past, some rice was donated to them from the States but the danger has grown so bad that that has been discontinued.

      Also more is needed for salaries and supplies. The teachers sometimes get almost no wages and have to resign to seek other jobs. However, they often stay because there are no other jobs.

      There are also medical needs. A daily vitamin would be great and worming medicine, and money to see a doctor. These expenses are not to be thought of as pouring money down a drain, but preparing a generation of educated Christians who can participate in their country to help lift it up.

      Kenya  ·  Founded by Beatrice Williams

      The Maisha Project

        No photographs here yet.

        The Maisha Project was founded by a Kenyan national, Beatrice, about 20 years ago. She was introduced to Jesus by a visiting team of Christians from the United States, and she soon decided to give her life to God. A team member who was impressed with Beatrice, because she had found and returned that team member’s wallet, paid for Beatrice’s education in Kenya as well as in the USA. Now, Beatrice works full time with Maisha, traveling back and forth between the USA and Kenya, where her family still lives. She has a sound Christian faith as well as a real talent for creating self-sustaining programs to meet the needs of her people.

        One of the many things Beatrice does is to head up a certified school of 600 children and tests prove it as one of the best in the region. The school includes 2 dorms that house some of the students who are considered at-risk if out in their communities. Other highlights include a student-run Christian program, a clean water well, a clean kitchen for meals for the children, a feeding program in a remote village, a chaplain’s program, a widows’ jobs program, a program that serves people in the slums who are affected by HIV, and a solar energy program so that students are able to study after dark (darkness on the equator is about 12 hours a day year-round). In addition, the students have recently started raising cows and chickens and growing their own fruits and vegetables.

        About 6 years ago, YOI helped Maisha start a small clinic which has developed into a fully self-sustaining hospital, certified by the Kenyan government. All this was accomplished by a strong staff who loves the Lord.

        2 years ago Maisha bought an unused school in an extremely rural area, 2 hours from any other medical facilities, and started converting it into another hospital, trusting God for the funds. As of August 2026 the hospital is almost finished and is starting to receive patients. Following up with their patients is important, so the Chaplains and staff at both hospitals are trained to visit their patients and their families by going to their homes and ministering to both the body and spirit. Also, in order to lend support to the hard-working national staff, various teams of visiting medical doctors and nurses come to Maisha’s hospitals to volunteer and to share the love of Jesus.

        Their needs are:

        • Funding for the second hospital until it becomes self-sustaining
        • Shipping fees for equipment and nutritious rice packets, which provide literally millions of meals
        • Volunteers for camp programs and for the 2 medical facilities
        • Salaries for staff
        • Other building and improvement projects
        • Sponsorships for children
