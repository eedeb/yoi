Webuye, western Kenya  ·  Pastor Elyves Situma

Grace Life Bible College

    No photographs here yet.

    Grace Life Bible College in Webuye, western Kenya; founder and president, Pastor Elyves Situma. A quote from their website, www.glbc.co.ke, says our mission is “equipping students with biblical knowledge, spiritual growth, and practical skills for ministry. We offer Theology, Church Music and Computer programs to empower you for God’s purpose.” “Our curriculum is grounded upon the authority of the inerrant Word of God.”

    We have worked with this passionate, creative man of prayer, Pastor Situma, for many years, entertaining him and his wife in our home and helping him when he is in the States to gather shipments of supplies to send back to Kenya. Twice now he has filled a container with 13,000 Christian books for his library, which has enabled the college to get certified as a college in Kenya. In addition, we helped him get a supply of tools to enable students and graduates to get side jobs. He also was able to get technical equipment. YOI has supported his ministry with finances, prayer, and frequent texts and phone calls. The very best way I could give you to understand this college is to refer you to their website. The drop-downs in the website show a lot of information about its doctrinal statement, history, programs, etc. One of the degrees is a seminary degree and graduates about 20 pastors a year.

    God planted into Pastor Situma’s heart the idea of a Biblical training college from statistics presented at an African Evangelical Association (AEA) conference that gave a conservative estimate that 85–90% of pastors and church leaders in Africa have little to no biblical and theological training required to effectively disciple their congregations.

    During Covid, Pastor Situma was not idle. He had up to 18 extra children in his home whose parents brought them to him because they could not feed them. When businesses were allowed to start back up, he shared his resources with people to replenish their shops and get back on their feet. But more than that, God gave him a vision to take sound doctrinal teaching to the remote villages. He wrote the curriculum, recruited several of his seminary graduates, and Youth Outreach bought 2 motorcycles. Enthusiasm in the villages was overwhelming, and the demand for classes grew quickly to 14 but had to be cut back to 8 due to the lack of resources. The class size is limited to a pastor and 40 of their congregants, including those who help with church leadership. The teacher stays for 2 days in one village, teaching classes and holding meetings individually with the leadership. He also checks the homework. If the student attends regularly and does all the homework, he or she receives a certificate. This course has created so much interest in the college that the college has had to add dorm and classroom space to accommodate the influx. The teacher eats and sleeps in the village and then moves on to his second village of the week. They receive about $6.00 a day if funds are available. After 4 days he returns to the college to study and prepare lessons, copy notebooks and handouts, and service the motorbike and other necessary preparations. They may also preach in a church on Sunday and work a side job to supplement the income. They often have families to support.

    The needs here are many.

    The college needs to grow in all ways, including more teachers and staff. (They welcome pastors to come teach for several weeks at a time.) They are renewing their accreditation, which is an expensive process. YOI has helped send 2 40-foot containers with over 23,000 books, along with shelves, tools for side jobs for some of the students, musical instruments, medical and technical equipment, and technical supplies.

    Montrouis, Haiti  ·  Pastor Pierre Gesner Fedelus

    Institution Puits Jacob/Pistos

      No photographs here yet.

      One of the national pastors YOI supports is in Haiti and runs a school for about 600 children in Montrouis, between St. Mark and Port-au-Prince, founded in 2015. We have known Pastor Pierre Gesner Fedelus (called Pastor Gesner) for years from our work in Haiti and his visits to the States. He is totally devoted to education and years ago started a school of all 12 grades for students coming in daily from the mountains and towns. Some of them pay a tuition and others cannot afford to pay. Recently 65% of the graduates passed the government achievement exams, and 100% of the 9th graders passed theirs! He also recently started a computer class with computers donated from the States. YOI helps with rice and teacher salaries. He comes to the States occasionally and preaches and gets some donations. Recently he linked up with a missionary aviation group to fly some supplies in.

      We have known and worked with Pastor Gesner in Haiti for many years. We have visited his school and Bible-believing church twice, and he has visited us in the States and stayed with us several times in our home. He is a hard-working man, dedicated to his students and staff, to his congregation and to his family. He has a very strong belief in prayer and faith in God to meet their needs. Youth Outreach International’s donors have been able to provide in a small way for some food and salaries for the 600 students and 40 staff.

      Life in Haiti has always been hard, but the current situation of gangs overtaking cities with the use of guns and theft has caused unbelievable hardship. Recently the gangs have come into his town, killing and stealing, and have come onto the school property and stolen the only car. Pastor Gesner moved his family further north, but he and the staff continue to teach. He goes back to his family by bus, which is unsafe, only 1 day a week. Some students are afraid to come to the school, so some brave teachers have gone out to the homes, realizing how important their education is.

      The school is fully accredited and is thought to be one of the best in the area. The difficulty is that there are so many parents who have no jobs and no way to pay tuition or feed their children. Students are coming so weak that they cannot sit up or study. The school tries to provide one plain rice meal 3 times a week with nothing added to it, like vegetables or protein or vitamins. The statistics I found claim that 5.7 million Haitians suffer severe food insecurity, which includes 1.2 million children under 5.

      YOI has helped families make some kind of a living rather than hoping someone will donate money to them. There has already been a microfinance program for about 20 years. Pastor Gesner and his staff proposed a goat program. He has had a few years of experience raising goats, he has some law experience so he can make legal contracts with each recipient, and he has trained with Hope International on how to run a microfinance program. He wants to start with 1 goat for each of 20 families and hire 2 paid supervisors to run it. They will purchase 20 pregnant goats for about $22 each, which will be kept at the individual home areas. According to the contracts, the newly pregnant kids will be moved along to new participants, and the original participant can keep one to grow its own flock. This will not produce enough income to pay a lot of tuition, but it should help buy some food and maybe a little income to start up other businesses. They also plan to collect the animal waste to help fertilize the poor soil to try to grow some food around their homes. They are preparing all the contracts and rules.

      The needs here are many.

      They have a grant to fully fund a budget of $4,000 to purchase 20 goats and 2 bucks, hire 2 supervisors, and plan for veterinarian fees, transportation and government permits for use of land, plus a few other items to care for the goats. If it is successful, they will want to fund another program.

      Food is a huge need but can be provided relatively cheaply for 600 students and 40 staff for rice with a few vegetables and oil 3x a week for about $2,500 a month, which is about $3.90 per person per month.

      Pastor Gesner is starting a second school in the mountains this fall (2026). He is starting from scratch, and he needs funding.

      His faith is huge!!!

      Kenya  ·  Founded by Beatrice Williams

      The Maisha Project

        No photographs here yet.

        The Maisha Project was founded by a Kenyan national, Beatrice, about 20 years ago. She was introduced to Jesus by a visiting team of Christians from the United States, and she soon decided to give her life to God. A team member who was impressed with Beatrice paid for her education in Kenya as well as in the USA. Beatrice works full time in this ministry, traveling back and forth between the US and Kenya, where her family still lives. She has a sound Christian faith as well as a real talent for creating self-sustaining programs to meet the needs of her people.

        Beatrice also heads up a certified school of 600 children. The school includes 2 dorms that house at-risk children, as well as a student-run Christian program, a clean water well, a clean kitchen for meals for children, a feeding program, a chaplain’s program, a widows’ jobs program, a program that serves people in the slums who are affected by HIV, and a solar energy program so that students are able to study after dark (darkness on the equator is about 12 hours year-round).

        In addition to raising cows and chickens at the school, and teaching students how to raise them, Beatrice has also started teaching horticulture classes, raising their own fruits and vegetables.

        About 6 years ago YOI helped Beatrice start a small clinic on the grounds of the school, which Beatrice has developed into a fully self-sustaining hospital, certified by the Kenyan government. She has a super strong staff.

        2 years ago she bought an unused school in a very rural area, 2 hours from any other medical facilities, and started building another hospital on faith for the funds. As of August 2026 it is almost finished and in use. Teams of medical doctors and nurses come to volunteer and share the love of Jesus. Chaplains and staff are all trained in follow-up and even continue to minister to the body and spirit as they visit patients in their homes.

        Her energy is boundless. She is sustained and guided by great faith and by the prayers of many people.

        Their needs are:

        • Funding for the hospital until it becomes self-sustaining
        • Shipping fees for equipment and nutritious rice packets, which provide literally millions of meals
        • Volunteers for camp programs and for the 2 medical facilities
        • Salaries for staff
        • Other building and improvement projects
        • Sponsorships for children
