Ministries

Three ministries, led by their own people.

Each is led by national Christians and their own staff, who know the language, customs and culture of the people they serve. We have known every one of them for years, visiting them in their own countries and hosting them in our home. Our part is to help, with donations, shipments and prayer.

Webuye, western Kenya  ·  Pastor Elyves Situma

Grace Life Bible College

A Bible college that trains pastors and church leaders, founded and led by its president, Pastor Elyves Situma.

    No photographs here yet.

    Why a Bible college

    God planted the idea in Pastor Situma’s heart through statistics presented at an African Evangelical Association conference. By a conservative estimate, 85 to 90 percent of pastors and church leaders in Africa have little or no biblical and theological training, which is what they need to disciple their congregations well.

    The college describes its mission in its own words:

    Equipping students with biblical knowledge, spiritual growth, and practical skills for ministry. We offer Theology, Church Music and Computer programs to empower you for God’s purpose. Our curriculum is grounded upon the authority of the inerrant Word of God.

    One of its degrees is a seminary degree, and the college graduates about twenty pastors a year. Its website is the best way to understand it, with much more on its doctrinal statement, history and programs.

    Working together

    We have worked with Pastor Situma, a passionate, creative man of prayer, for many years. We have hosted him and his wife in our home, and helped him gather shipments of supplies when he is in the States. Two 40-foot containers have gone to Kenya carrying more than 23,000 Christian books for the college library, which helped it gain its certification as a college in Kenya. The containers also carried shelves, musical instruments, medical and technical equipment and supplies, and tools so that students and graduates can earn money from side jobs. YOI also supports the ministry with finances, prayer, and frequent texts and phone calls.

    Taking the teaching to the villages

    Pastor Situma was not idle during Covid. He took in as many as eighteen extra children whose parents brought them to him because they could not feed them. When businesses were allowed to open again, he shared his own resources so people could restock their shops and get back on their feet. And God gave him a vision to take sound doctrinal teaching out to remote villages. He wrote the curriculum and recruited several of his seminary graduates, and Youth Outreach bought two motorcycles.

    The enthusiasm in the villages was overwhelming. Demand quickly grew to fourteen classes, though a lack of resources meant cutting back to eight. Each class is a pastor and up to forty of his congregation, including those who help lead the church. A teacher spends two days in a village, teaching classes, meeting individually with the leadership and checking homework. He eats and sleeps there, then moves on to his second village of the week. Students who attend regularly and complete all the homework receive a certificate.

    After four days on the road, the teacher returns to the college to study and prepare lessons, copy notebooks and handouts, and service the motorbike. Teachers receive about $6 a day when funds are available. Many also preach on Sunday and work a side job, and they often have families to support. The village classes have stirred so much interest in the college itself that it has had to add dormitory and classroom space.

    Montrouis, Haiti  ·  Pastor Pierre Gesner Fedelus

    Institution Puits Jacob

    A school of all twelve grades for about 600 children, founded in 2015 by Pastor Pierre Gesner Fedelus, known to everyone as Pastor Gesner.

      No photographs here yet.

      The school

      Montrouis lies between Saint-Marc and Port-au-Prince. Pastor Gesner is totally devoted to education, and started the school for students who come in each day from the mountains and the towns. Some pay tuition; others cannot. The school is fully accredited and thought to be one of the best in the area. Recently 65 percent of its graduates passed the government achievement exams, and every one of its 9th graders passed theirs. He has also started a computer class with computers donated from the States.

      We have known and worked with Pastor Gesner for many years. We have visited his school and his Bible-believing church twice, and he has stayed in our home several times on visits to the States, where he preaches and raises support. He is a hard-working man, dedicated to his students and staff, his congregation and his family, with a very strong belief in prayer and faith in God to meet their needs. Through YOI, donors have been able to provide, in a small way, some of the food and salaries for 600 students and 40 staff. He has also linked up with a missionary aviation group to fly supplies in.

      Teaching through the violence

      Life in Haiti has always been hard, but gangs taking over cities with guns and theft have caused unbelievable hardship. Recently the gangs came into Montrouis, killing and stealing, and came onto the school property, where they stole the school’s only car. Pastor Gesner moved his family further north, but he and the staff continue to teach. He goes back to his family by bus one day a week, which is unsafe. Some students are afraid to come to school, so some brave teachers have gone out to teach them in their homes, knowing how important their education is.

      Feeding students

      So many parents have no jobs and no way to pay tuition or feed their children that students arrive too weak to sit up or study. The school tries to provide one plain meal of rice three times a week, with nothing added to it: no vegetables, protein or vitamins. By one estimate, 5.7 million Haitians suffer severe food insecurity, including 1.2 million children under five.

      Food is a huge need, but it can be provided cheaply. Rice with a few vegetables and oil, three times a week, for all 600 students and 40 staff costs about $2,500 a month, or about $3.90 per person.

      Helping families earn a living

      YOI wants to help families make some kind of a living rather than depend on donations. A microfinance program has already been running for about twenty years, and Pastor Gesner has trained with Hope International in how to run one. Now he and his staff are starting a goat program. Pastor Gesner has raised goats before, and his experience with the law means he can draw up a contract with each family.

      Twenty families will each receive a pregnant goat, bought for about $22, overseen by two paid supervisors who know the families. When the kids are weaned at three months, they are bred and passed along to new families, and each original family keeps one to start its own small flock. It will not pay much tuition, but it should help buy food and perhaps start other small businesses, and the manure will help fertilize poor soil for home gardens. A grant fully funds the $4,000 first round: 20 goats and 2 bucks, the supervisors, veterinary fees, transport and government permits for the land. If it works, they will want to fund another.

      A second school

      This fall (2026), Pastor Gesner is starting a second school in the mountains, from scratch, and it needs funding. His faith is huge.

      Kenya  ·  Beatrice Williams

      Maisha Project

      Founded about twenty years ago by Beatrice Williams, a Kenyan with a real talent for building programs that sustain themselves.

        No photographs here yet.

        Beatrice

        Beatrice was introduced to Jesus by a visiting team of Christians from the United States, and soon decided to give her life to God. A member of that team, impressed with her, paid for her education in Kenya and in the USA. She now works full time in the ministry, travelling back and forth between the States and Kenya, where her family still lives. She has a sound Christian faith and a real talent for creating self-sustaining programs to meet the needs of her people. Her energy is boundless, sustained and guided by great faith and by the prayers of many people.

        The school

        Beatrice heads a certified school of 600 children. It includes two dormitories that house at-risk children, a student-run Christian program, a clean-water well, a clean kitchen and a feeding program, a chaplain’s program, a jobs program for widows, a program serving people in the slums who are affected by HIV, and a solar energy program so that students can study after dark. On the equator, darkness lasts about twelve hours all year round.

        The school raises cows and chickens and teaches students how to raise them, and Beatrice has started horticulture classes, so the school grows its own fruit and vegetables.

        Two hospitals

        About six years ago, YOI helped Beatrice start a small clinic on the school grounds. She has developed it into a fully self-sustaining hospital, certified by the Kenyan government, with a super strong staff.

        Two years ago she bought an unused school in a very rural area, two hours from any other medical facility, and began building a second hospital on faith that the funds would come. As of August 2026 it is almost finished and already in use. Teams of doctors and nurses come to volunteer and share the love of Jesus. Chaplains and staff are all trained in follow-up, and continue to minister to body and spirit as they visit patients in their homes.
